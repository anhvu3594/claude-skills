# Before/After Resume Transformations

Real, anonymized resume refactors from engineers who weren't getting callbacks — then started landing interviews after the rewrite. Names, companies, schools, and dates are altered. Use these to recognize *patterns* of improvement, not to copy bullets verbatim. The candidate is the same person doing the same work in both versions — only the framing changed.

## Table of contents

1. [Software Engineer, 2 years — Facebook Rotational Program](#1-software-engineer-2-years)

The recurring lesson across all five: **same experience, stronger framing.** Lead with impact, add numbers, use active verbs, name technologies, cut what doesn't sell, and use a single-column top-down layout that a recruiter can scan in 10 seconds.

## 1. Software Engineer, 2 years

Applying to Facebook's Rotational Engineering Program (full-stack, JavaScript/React/PHP, 2+ years).

**What was wrong with the original:**
- Overly generous margins shrank the usable space.
- Inconsistent fonts, sizes, and alignment across sections — looked sloppy.
- Every previous company name was a hyperlink, so the recruiter's eye was pulled to company names instead of titles and dates. Company website links add no value — a recruiter can search the name.
- Bullets described activity without impact: *"Built a mobile app"* with no sense of what was challenging or valuable.
- A 3-month role was split into two sections to show a fast promotion — but such a short stint is better merged or omitted than fragmented.

**Before → After bullets:**

> BEFORE: "Re-architected and maintaining a RESTful API using PHP/Laravel with third-party API integrations such as zoom-rooms to enrich customer experience."
> AFTER: "Improved customer conversion by 30% of the co-working platform by implementing recurring payments and integrating Zoom rooms."

> BEFORE: "Built a mobile app (MennoInnova) for educating farmers enrolled in the Farmer Business Management programme. This was built with Kotlin, and SqlLite Database."
> AFTER: "Built an app for educating cocoa farmers enrolled in the Farmer Business School programme, using Kotlin and Room database."

> BEFORE: "Designed and implemented an API for interfacing the MennoInnova mobile application using PHP/Lumen, AWS(RDS, Elasticbeanstalk), PostMan."
> AFTER: "Designed and implemented APIs that powered our 10 mobile applications using PHP, Lumen, Laravel, AWS RDS, AWS Elasticbeanstalk and PostMan."

**Key moves:** Tailored skills order to the job (PHP and JavaScript first, since the role named them). Replaced scattered company links with two deliberate links to standout blog articles the candidate wanted the hiring manager to read. Cut repetition while keeping the same information.

## 2. Machine Learning Engineer, 5 years

Original was a 3-page resume with a dark, hard-to-read template. Refactored to 1 page; the candidate then got responses from companies that had ignored them before and landed an offer.

**What was wrong with the original:**
- An "instant turn-off" template with heavy black header bars that drew the eye for no reason.
- First page led with self-ratings (Python 4/5, JavaScript 3/5). Self-ratings only hurt: a hiring manager reads "3/5 JavaScript" as "not proficient," which is rarely what the candidate means. Drop ratings entirely.
- Each role spent as many words describing *the company* as describing the candidate's own work.
- Paragraphs instead of bullets — a reader who skims the first sentence (about the company) skips the rest.
- The real depth was on pages 2–3, which most recruiters never reached.

**Before → After bullets:**

> BEFORE: "Help build up tooling and infrastructure for Data Science team for model deployment, management and performance monitoring."
> AFTER: "Created infrastructure needed for development, staging, and production within the Data Science pipeline."

> BEFORE (paragraph about the company + vague duties)
> AFTER: "Reduced 50% of costs on cloud hosting by building an in-house scaling tool to manage cloud resources."

> AFTER: "Improved the speed of Spark clustering algorithms whilst reducing the cost by building the infrastructure to use AWS EMR (Elastic Map Reduce)."

**Still could be better (noted even after refactor):** name specific technologies *inside* each bullet, and add numbers wherever possible (e.g. "deployed 4 microservices serving 100 RPS" rather than "created a web application"). Impact + numbers + named tech is the goal.

## 3. Backend Developer, 6 years

Payroll-software backend engineer applying to larger companies. Decent starting resume, refactored for more callbacks; ended up taking a senior role at a startup.

**What was wrong with the original:**
- No location listed — recruiters assume "out of country / needs visa" when location is missing. The candidate was in Vancouver; listing it removes doubt.
- Technologies dumped in one long comma-separated run, not ordered by relevance to the target role.
- Too much bolding (all skills bolded, random results bolded, "W4 forms" bolded) — drew attention to the wrong places.
- Some bullets had specifics; others were vague ("created troubleshooting tools that improved efficiency by 30%" — using what? leading whom? how many users?).
- Education had no dates (confusing — did they graduate?).
- Dates buried inline instead of right-aligned and scannable.
- Delivered as a `.docx` instead of PDF.

**Before → After bullets:**

> BEFORE: "Led development of two separate third party interface development projects for two of our biggest clients with Workday and ADP, which were completed on time with 45% cost reduction."
> AFTER: "Reduced operation costs by 45% for a client by developing third party interfaces using Java, Workday and ADP."

> BEFORE: "Automated error handling process of our restful API services that reduced one of our clients support ticket rate by 80%."
> AFTER: "Reduced support tickets by 80% for a large client by automating error handling on their restful API."

> BEFORE: "Created troubleshooting tools that improved support team's efficiency by more than 30%."
> AFTER: "Improved the support team's throughput by 30% by building troubleshooting tools using Javascript."

**Key moves:** Lead each bullet with the *result* (the percentage), then explain how. Reorder the skills section to put the languages the target jobs ask for first. Add education dates. Right-align dates. Drop months on roles 4+ years old. Mention leading a small team — a detail previously left off.

## 4. Remote Software Engineer, 8 years

Senior engineer in Europe targeting remote roles, barely getting responses. The original "broke several unwritten CV rules." After refactor, response rate jumped and they chose among multiple offers.

**What was wrong with the original:**
- Two-column infographic template that started with **Education**, with the real experience hidden on page 2.
- A **photo** — introduces bias; remove it.
- Irrelevant skills listed: "Leadership," "Cat-B driving license" for a remote dev job.
- Star ratings (max 4/5, one star on Node.js) made an actual expert look weak.
- **Work experience in the wrong chronological order** — oldest first on page 1. Always reverse-chronological.
- Described *what* they did, not the results, and used passive language.

**Refactored version (single-column, top-down):**

> AFTER skills line: "Expert in Python and C++ and a wide variety of frameworks for these languages, for example: Flask, Selenium, Numpy, Qt5, Boost, POCO"

> AFTER: "Up to 10 times increase in store throughput in some cases, with up to 50% decrease in manual labor requirements. Technologies used: Python (with Flask, Selenium and Numpy), C++ (with Qt framework), Linux and Cloud architecture, JavaScript, git, CMake and qmake."

> AFTER: "Increased performance of stereo-camera lane detector solution by 100%, enabling it to run on an nVidia DrivePX2 or Jetson embedded hardware with acceptable reaction time in less than 2 weeks. Technologies used: C++(14), Qt, Python, CMake, nVidia SDKs, git."

**Still could be better (noted even after refactor):** use active verbs ("Created" not "Creating"), clean up date formatting ("Jun 2019 – Mar 2020" reads better than "2019 summer – 2020 spring"), don't repeat the header on page 2, and move Education further down for a senior candidate.

## 5. SRE / Systems Engineer, 20 years

Systems admin with a PhD, employed 10+ years, applying for SRE roles. Original was the same CV from 2010 with new jobs appended. After refactor, got the SRE offer they wanted.

**What was wrong with the original:**
- Dated, table-heavy template that *looked* old — risks the subconscious bias of "won't keep up with new tech" (untrue here; they used HAProxy, Prometheus, ELK).
- Long multi-paragraph intro with few specifics.
- Irrelevant key skills on page 1 ("excellent interpersonal skills," "training course design").
- 10 years at one employer with **no promotion shown** — leaves the recruiter wondering if they stagnated.
- Irrelevant older roles (training consultant 2004–2008, early sysadmin jobs) padding the resume.
- No results; clichés like "excellent communication."

**Refactored version (single-column, two pages — appropriate here):**

> AFTER: "Leads a team of 3 administrators, managing 300 physical machines and private cloud of 150 virtual machines across 2 data centres, providing virtualisation resources and 300TB of storage."

> AFTER: "Identified frequent manual cluster node remediation as a source of toil. Improved overall cluster uptime from 90% to 95% by leading the creation of auto-remediation tooling for common issues."

> AFTER: "Reduced runtime of cluster node health-checks from 3 seconds to 0.3 seconds by introducing caches of system hardware configuration."

> AFTER: "Wrote 70% of our cluster automation tooling in the past 5 years. Reviewed the remaining 30%."

**Key moves:** Promotion now called out (promoted after 2 years, now leads the HPC team). Older pre-MSc roles cut entirely — a hiring manager doesn't care what someone did before a decade of strong results. Projects (with GitHub links), conference talks, and teaching round out a strong story. **Two pages is fine here** because the candidate is senior, applies in the UK where one-page norms are looser, and has enough meaningful content to fill them — page count should follow content, not a rule.

## What every refactor had in common

- **Lead with impact, then explain how.** Numbers (%, RPS, team size, users, cost) go up front where they grab attention.
- **Active verbs, "I" not "we".** Led, Built, Reduced, Designed, Improved — not "Responsible for" or "Working on."
- **Name the technologies inside the bullet**, not just in a skills list.
- **Cut what doesn't sell the target role:** company descriptions, irrelevant old jobs, clichés, self-ratings, photos, driving licenses.
- **Single-column, top-down, reverse-chronological**, with dates right-aligned and standing out.
- **Show promotions** so a long tenure reads as growth, not stagnation.
- **PDF, not docx.** Tailor per role rather than sending one generic resume everywhere.
